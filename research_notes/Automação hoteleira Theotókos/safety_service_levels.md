# Automação hoteleira Theotókos: níveis de serviço e segurança

## 1. Obrigações e expectativas aplicáveis

### Takeaway
Theotókos Flats should treat the automated guest-service system as a customer-facing part of the accommodation business, not as an autonomous decision-maker. The safe baseline is: transparent booking and pricing; secure payment redirection; privacy-by-design; accessible and multilingual communication; a staffed escalation path; and emergency instructions that never depend solely on the bot.

### Cited Findings
- EU consumer rules require traders to provide clear, understandable pre-contract information, including the main characteristics of the service, total price and additional charges, payment arrangements, performance conditions, and cancellation terms; fixed-date accommodation is generally excluded from the ordinary 14-day withdrawal right, so the accommodation’s cancellation policy must be explicit before booking. [European Commission — Consumer rights](https://commission.europa.eu/law/law-topic/consumer-protection-law/consumer-contract-law/consumer-rights-directive_en)
- The European Consumer Centres Network explains that accommodation information should accurately describe the property, price, facilities, booking terms and remedies when the accommodation is not as advertised. [ECC-Net — Hotels](https://www.eccnet.eu/consumer-rights/what-are-my-consumer-rights/travel-and-passenger-rights/accommodation/hotels)
- EU Regulation 2024/1028 establishes a framework for collecting and sharing data about short-term rental services and applies from 20 May 2026; its scope and any Greek registration requirements must be checked against Theotókos Flats’ legal classification. [EUR-Lex — Regulation (EU) 2024/1028](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2024/1028/oj)
- GDPR requires lawfulness, fairness and transparency; purpose limitation; data minimisation; accuracy; storage limitation; integrity and confidentiality; and accountability. [EUR-Lex — GDPR, Article 5](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2016/679/oj)
- GDPR requires controllers to provide information about purposes, legal bases, recipients, retention and rights, and requires appropriate technical and organisational security measures. [EUR-Lex — GDPR, Articles 13, 30 and 32](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2016/679/oj)
- A controller must notify a personal-data breach to the supervisory authority without undue delay and, where feasible, within 72 hours after becoming aware of it when the GDPR threshold is met; processors must notify the controller without undue delay. [EUR-Lex — GDPR, Articles 33–34](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2016/679/oj)
- The European Data Protection Board states that a processor may process personal data only on documented instructions from the controller and that controller–processor arrangements must address security, confidentiality, assistance, deletion/return and audits. [EDPB — Guidelines 07/2020 on controller/processor concepts](https://www.edpb.europa.eu/our-work-tools/our-documents/guidelines/guidelines-072020-concepts-controller-and-processor-gdpr_en)
- The EU Web Accessibility Directive applies to public-sector websites and mobile applications, while the European Accessibility Act covers specified private-sector products and services; the legal applicability of a particular private accommodation interface depends on the service and national transposition, so Theotókos Flats should not assume that a private booking page is exempt without legal review. [European Commission — European Accessibility Act](https://commission.europa.eu/strategy-and-policy/policies/justice-and-fundamental-rights/disability/strategy-rights-persons-disabilities/european-accessibility-act_en); [EUR-Lex — Directive (EU) 2019/882](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/dir/2019/882/oj)
- WCAG 2.2 provides testable accessibility success criteria for perceivable, operable, understandable and robust web content; W3C advises using WCAG as the shared technical reference even where a legal rule names a different version. [W3C — WCAG 2.2](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/)
- NIST’s hospitality-specific SP 1800-27 describes property-management-system risks involving guest information, payment information and operational disruption, and demonstrates segmentation, strong authentication, monitoring, logging and protected interfaces as mitigations. [NIST NCCoE — Securing Property Management Systems](https://www.nccoe.nist.gov/publication/1800-27/VolA/index.html)
- PCI DSS is a payment-card security standard for entities that store, process or transmit cardholder data; using a hosted payment page or tokenized gateway can reduce the card-data environment but does not remove the merchant’s responsibility to understand and validate its compliance scope. [PCI Security Standards Council — PCI DSS](https://www.pcisecuritystandards.org/standards/pci-dss/)
- The Greek Data Protection Authority is the national supervisory authority for GDPR matters in Greece and provides complaint and breach-related information for data subjects and controllers. [Hellenic DPA](https://www.dpa.gr/en)
- Emergency callers in Greece can use the single European emergency number 112; the system should therefore display 112 prominently and tell guests to call emergency services directly rather than waiting for an automated response. [European Commission — 112 emergency number](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/policies/112)

### Inferences
- Because Theotókos Flats may receive guests who book through direct channels and platforms, the same canonical price, inventory, cancellation and accessibility facts should feed every channel; a chatbot must not invent or silently alter contractual terms.
- The safest payment design is a redirect or embedded hosted checkout from a PCI-compliant provider, with the guest-service layer receiving only a payment status and provider token, never the full card number or security code.
- Identity documents, chat transcripts, access codes and incident records should have separate retention rules; “keep everything indefinitely” would conflict with storage limitation unless a documented legal purpose exists.
- Every high-risk interaction (payment dispute, lockout, injury, fire, suspected fraud, discrimination, accessibility failure or data-rights request) should create a human-owned case with timestamps and an audit trail.

### Gaps
- The property’s exact legal status, municipality, licence, registration number, fire-safety file, tax regime, occupancy limits and use of booking platforms were not provided; Greek counsel or the competent tourism authority should verify property-specific obligations.
- No reliable primary source located in this pass establishes one universal Greek deposit percentage or cancellation rule for every accommodation category; the system must implement the actual signed terms and obtain local legal confirmation before hard-coding limits.

## 2. Recommended service levels and operating controls

### Takeaway
Use automation for speed and consistency, but define service levels around safe completion, not just response time. Theotókos Flats should provide 24/7 automated acknowledgement, immediate emergency routing, rapid human takeover for defined risk categories, resilient offline instructions, and a tested fallback for outages.

### Cited Findings
- NIST CSF 2.0 organizes cybersecurity outcomes around Govern, Identify, Protect, Detect, Respond and Recover, which supports an explicit owner, risk register, monitoring, incident response and recovery plan for the guest-service system. [NIST — Cybersecurity Framework 2.0](https://www.nist.gov/cyberframework)
- NIST SP 800-61 Rev. 2 describes incident response as preparation, detection and analysis, containment/eradication/recovery, and post-incident activity; these phases are suitable for guest-service outages, account compromise and data incidents. [NIST — SP 800-61 Rev. 2](https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/sp/800/61/r2/final)
- NIST SP 800-63B recommends phishing-resistant or otherwise strong authenticator practices for appropriate assurance levels and supports risk-based authentication rather than relying on knowledge questions alone. [NIST — SP 800-63B](https://pages.nist.gov/800-63-3/sp800-63b.html)
- ISO 22301 defines requirements for a business-continuity management system, including preparation for, response to and recovery from disruptive incidents; ISO 27001 defines requirements for an information-security management system. [ISO — ISO 22301](https://www.iso.org/standard/75106.html); [ISO — ISO/IEC 27001](https://www.iso.org/standard/27001)
- WCAG requires keyboard operability, sufficient contrast, visible focus, understandable labels and error identification among its testable criteria; these are directly relevant to booking, payment handoff, check-in and complaint forms. [W3C — WCAG 2.2](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/)
- The European emergency number 112 is intended for urgent situations requiring police, fire or medical assistance; a property interface must not delay or gate access to that number. [European Commission — 112](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/policies/112)
- NIST’s hospitality profile identifies segmentation between property-management, point-of-sale, access-control, guest Wi-Fi and vendor systems as a way to limit compromise and lateral movement. [NIST NCCoE — SP 1800-27](https://www.nccoe.nist.gov/publication/1800-27/VolA/index.html)

### Inferences
- Publish two service-level classes: **critical safety** (fire, gas smell, medical emergency, violence, lockout with vulnerability, missing child, active security threat) and **ordinary service** (reservation changes, amenity questions, invoices, checkout). Critical safety should bypass normal queues and route to 112 plus the on-call human; ordinary issues can use asynchronous targets.
- The bot should ask only the minimum clarifying question needed to route safely. It should never troubleshoot smoke, gas, serious injury or violence for multiple turns.
- Check-in/out automation should have a “fail safe” path: a visible local contact number, physical instructions, backup access process, and a human override. No guest should be stranded solely because identity verification, internet, battery, vendor API or lock integration failed.
- Theotókos Flats should monitor not only uptime but also wrong-answer rate, failed handoffs, duplicate charges, access-code errors, unresolved complaints and emergency drill performance.

### Gaps
- No property-specific staffing roster, response hours, lock hardware, network design, PMS/channel manager, payment provider, language mix, accessibility audit or emergency plan was supplied; numeric targets below are recommended acceptance criteria, not claims about legal minimums.

## 3. Measurable acceptance criteria for Theotókos Flats

### Takeaway
Before launch, Theotókos Flats should pass a scenario-based acceptance test covering every guest journey and failure mode. The criteria below are operational recommendations derived from the cited consumer, privacy, accessibility, continuity and hospitality-security sources; they should be approved by the owner, DPO/privacy adviser and local safety professional.

### Cited Findings
- GDPR accountability requires the controller to be able to demonstrate compliance, making documented tests, records of processing, vendor agreements and incident evidence important. [EUR-Lex — GDPR, Article 5(2)](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2016/679/oj)
- GDPR data-subject rights include access, rectification, erasure, restriction, objection and portability in the circumstances defined by the Regulation; the system therefore needs a traceable intake and response workflow. [EUR-Lex — GDPR, Articles 12–22](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2016/679/oj)
- WCAG success criteria are testable and can be verified with automated tools plus manual keyboard, screen-reader, zoom and contrast testing. [W3C — WCAG 2.2](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/)
- PCI DSS applies to the cardholder-data environment and requires organizations to define and protect the relevant scope; reducing card data handled by Theotókos Flats is therefore a defensible risk-reduction objective. [PCI Security Standards Council — PCI DSS](https://www.pcisecuritystandards.org/standards/pci-dss/)
- NIST incident-response guidance emphasizes preparation, detection, response, recovery and lessons learned, supporting recurring drills and evidence-based remediation. [NIST — SP 800-61 Rev. 2](https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/sp/800/61/r2/final)

### Inferences
- **Reservations and pricing:** 100% of sampled booking journeys show dates, occupancy, room/unit identity, mandatory fees/taxes, optional fees, payment timing, cancellation/no-show terms, accessibility facts, contact route and final payable total before confirmation; a weekly reconciliation finds zero mismatches between the direct channel, PMS and confirmation email.
- **Payments:** 0% of application logs, analytics events, support transcripts or databases contain full PAN or CVV; 100% of payment attempts return an idempotent status; duplicate-charge rate is 0 in a 100-case test; refunds are traceable to the original transaction and a human can suspend automated charging within 5 minutes.
- **Check-in/out:** 99.9% monthly availability for the guest-facing service excluding planned maintenance; 95% of valid self-check-ins complete within 3 minutes; 100% of failed verifications expose a human fallback within 60 seconds; no door/access code is sent until the reservation and payment/authorisation state is valid; checkout produces an itemized receipt and preserves a dispute route.
- **Accessibility:** the booking, payment handoff, check-in and complaint journeys pass WCAG 2.2 AA automated checks with zero critical violations and pass manual keyboard-only, 200% zoom, visible-focus, screen-reader and colour-contrast tests; all critical emergency text is available as text and audio/visual alternatives where the interface supports them; accessibility claims about the flat are verified on-site rather than generated by the model.
- **Multilingual service:** Greek and English are available end-to-end for booking, payment explanation, check-in/out, complaints and emergencies; every translated critical message is human-reviewed; the system identifies the active language, avoids translating legal terms or emergency addresses ambiguously, and offers a human interpreter/English-or-Greek fallback within 10 minutes during staffed hours.
- **Complaints:** 100% of complaints receive an immediate case ID, timestamp, category, expected next step and human escalation option; safety, discrimination, accessibility and payment complaints are acknowledged in 5 minutes and assigned to a human in 15 minutes; ordinary complaints receive a substantive response within 1 business day; closure requires guest-visible resolution or documented reason.
- **Emergencies:** messages containing fire, smoke, gas, serious injury, violence, threat, missing child or “call emergency” trigger an interruptive response in under 5 seconds that displays “Call 112 now,” the property address, and the on-call contact; the system never claims to have contacted emergency services unless an auditable integration confirms it; quarterly drills achieve 100% correct routing and zero unsafe scripted troubleshooting.
- **Privacy:** 100% of data-collection screens link to a layered notice; every field has a documented purpose, legal basis, retention period and recipient; rights requests are acknowledged within 2 business days and tracked to the statutory response workflow; processor inventory and data-processing agreements cover the PMS, channel manager, chatbot, analytics, locks, email/SMS and payment provider; breach playbooks support controller assessment and the GDPR 72-hour supervisory-notification deadline where applicable.
- **Security and continuity:** MFA protects all privileged accounts; access is least-privilege and reviewed quarterly; guest Wi-Fi is segmented from PMS/POS/locks; critical logs are tamper-evident and retained according to the approved schedule; restore tests meet an owner-approved RTO of 4 hours and RPO of 24 hours; one live failover exercise per quarter demonstrates manual check-in, payment exception handling and emergency contact availability.
- **Model governance:** a test set of at least 200 representative conversations is run before each material release; unsupported, unsafe or legally sensitive prompts must hand off rather than guess; target grounded-answer accuracy is at least 98% for approved property facts, with 0 fabricated emergency numbers, prices, cancellation rules or accessibility claims; all changes have an owner, version, approval and rollback path.

### Gaps
- These thresholds are proposed acceptance criteria, not statutory safe harbours. The owner should calibrate them to occupancy, staffing, property size, insurer requirements, supplier SLAs and a documented risk assessment.
- A final go-live decision still requires verification of Greek accommodation licensing, fire and evacuation arrangements, local accessibility conditions, tax/receipt processes, identity-reporting obligations, payment-acquirer terms and the exact contracts of every processor and platform.
